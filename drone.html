Autonomous Aerial Object Retrieval

Goal
Language-commanded object retrieval from the air
Platform
SO101 arm mounted on a lightweight drone
Compute
Onboard NVIDIA Jetson Nano running SmolVLA

This project explores the limits of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in moving, high-vibration environments. The goal is for an aerial robot to carry out retrieval tasks from natural-language commands, such as "Pick up the red block."

Hardware-software co-design

Mounting an SO101 arm on a lightweight drone was a significant mechatronic challenge. We had to optimize the thrust-to-weight ratio while keeping enough edge compute to run inference onboard.

  • Edge computing: Integrated an NVIDIA Jetson Nano for real-time vision processing and SmolVLA inference.
  • Dynamic stability: Solved the center-of-gravity shifts caused by a heavy robotic arm moving relative to the drone's frame.
  • Power management: Balanced high-capacity battery needs against the strict weight limits of an aerial platform.
  • Unconventional arm control: Explored several ways to control a 6-DOF arm with an unusual range of motion, mounted upside down under the drone.

Reflection

This project was riddled with roadblocks from start to finish. I reflashed the Jetson more times than I can count, ran into compatibility issues with seemingly every software installation, and spent weeks exploring different ways to control a 6-degree-of-freedom arm with an unusual range of motion, mounted upside down on the drone. Nothing about it was straightforward.

What this project really taught me was resilience. There was never an easy solution — problems surfaced constantly, and solving one often meant uncovering ten more hiding behind it. It tested my patience in a way none of my other projects had, and there were plenty of late nights where I felt further behind than when I'd started.

But that frustration is exactly what made the eventual successes feel earned. Working through a project with no clear playbook forced me to get comfortable with uncertainty and to keep iterating even when progress felt invisible. Going forward, I know that the projects worth doing rarely go smoothly, and that persistence through the roadblocks is often the real work.
